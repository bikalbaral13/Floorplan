id: read-openings-and-fixtures-by-zoom
name: Read doors, windows, labels and furniture from zoomed crops
category: Floorplan Tracer
when: After the wall bands are measured — to classify each gap in a wall and to place every fixture with its true footprint and rotation.
tools: [Bash, Read]
requires: [measure-wall-bands-from-pixels]
---

Pixels tell you where a wall is interrupted; they do not tell you whether that interruption is a hinged door, a sliding door, a window or just an open edge between dining and living. That is read from the drawing, by you, at magnification.

1. **Crop with a labelled grid, don't squint.** Cut each room out at 3–4× (nearest-neighbour) and draw gridlines every 10 image-px with the x/y value printed on every 50th line (`grid_crops.py` in the scripts file). A furniture edge is then read as "x 650, y 163" directly off the crop instead of being converted back from crop pixels by hand — that conversion is where the 3BHK trace lost 5–10 px per object. A whole-plan view at 600 px hides door arcs and mirrors the swing side.
2. **Doors.** A quarter-circle arc + a leaf line is a hinged door. Hinge = the end of the gap the arc is centred on — check it by distance: the arc's two ends must be ~equidistant from the hinge you picked. Swing = the side of the wall the arc lies on. Record both relative to the segment's start→end direction you will author (see the door maths in `author-plan-graph-in-image-pixels`), and `leaves: "Double"` when two arcs meet. A wide gap with two parallel thin lines and no arc is a sliding door — author it as a Window object with `sillHeight: 0`, `operation: "Sliding"` so it draws as glazing, and label it.
3. **Windows.** A gap in an EXTERIOR wall with a thin double or triple line, usually with a rectangular chajja/sill box drawn outside it. Width is the light run's span. Toilet windows are short and high — `sillHeight 1.5–1.8, panes 1`; bedroom windows spanning a wall — `panes 3`. Do not trace the chajja box (see the authoring file — it closes into a sliver room on import).
4. **Open boundaries.** A gap with no leaf and no glazing (kitchen → living, kitchen → utility, foyer → living) is an OPENING: record its two jamb pixels and the thickness of the wall it interrupts, because it is authored as a wall-thickness piece hosting `opening.json`, not as a dashed line (see `author-plan-graph-in-image-pixels`). A balcony's open side drawn as a thin line is an opening too — vacant, drawn as a dotted line, no parapet. Only a divider that is not a wall gap (a niche edge) stays a dashed line.
5. **Labels and printed sizes.** Transcribe the room name exactly as printed (`BEDROOM-2`, `LIVING / DINING`), because the label is what the user matches against the picture. Unlabelled rooms get a sensible name (`DUCT`, `NICHE`, `SERVICE SHAFT`).
6. **Brochure symbol legend** (Indian developer plans): a rectangle filled with parallel stripes along a wall = wardrobe; a rectangle with a `V` inside on a wall = TV; a narrow rectangle with one diagonal line = dressing-table mirror (its table is the plain rectangle next to it, with a stool); a box with a dashed `X` = duct / service shaft (a space, not furniture); a circle beside a bed = round nightstand; an oval in a white counter = washbasin on a vanity; four small circles in a counter = hob; a hatched square in a counter = sink with drainer; a square with a smaller square inside in the utility = washing machine; a rectangle with a small inner square outside the plan = the compass.
7. **Furniture.** For each item record: kind → library object (bed, sofa, armchair, table, chair, wardrobe, cabinet for kitchen runs, nightstand, tv-unit, dressing-table, stool, shoe-rack, toilet, washbasin, kitchen-sink, stove, refrigerator, washing-machine, north-arrow — all under `Objects/Architectural/`); footprint centre in image px; footprint length × width in px; rotation. Rugs are `space`-hosted in the library, not node-hosted — skip them or attach them to the room space. Rotation is degrees with the object's long axis along `(cos r, sin r)` on the y-down canvas. **Verified conventions** (from the baked footprints): objects whose length runs AWAY from the wall — bed, toilet, chair — keep their head / cistern / back at the START of the axis, so `r = 90` puts a bed's pillows at the top of the screen and `r = 180` at the right, a toilet's cistern at the top for `r = 90` and at the bottom for `r = 270`, a chair facing down the axis. Objects whose length runs ALONG the wall — sofa, tv-unit, wardrobe, cabinet, dressing-table, refrigerator, kitchen-sink, stove — keep their back on the side `rotate((0, 1), r)`: `r = 0` back at the bottom, `r = 90` back at the left, `r = 180` back at the top, `r = 270` back at the right. Confirm on the overlay proof anyway: the proof draws the baked footprint, not a dot.
8. **Wall-hung things** (a TV over a low unit, a mirror) either hang on the wall segment (an attachment with `anchorDir: "Top-Center"` at a `t` along the wall) or sit as a `tv-unit` node with `base: "Wall-hung"` a few px inside the wall face so the symbol draws in front of the band, not inside it.

Keep a per-room checklist as you go: every gap in `walls_measured.json` must end up classified as door / window / open / niche, and every visible fixture must have a line in the build script.
